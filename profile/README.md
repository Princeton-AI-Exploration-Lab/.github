# AI Exploration Lab

**Princeton Data and Intelligent Systems, Fall 2026**

The AI Exploration Lab (AEL) is a semester-long program in which about 30
Princeton undergraduates run experiments on AI models. You take a research kit,
design a controlled experiment, collect your data, and present a poster at the
December symposium.

This page is for the Fall 2026 cohort. If you are interested in a future cohort,
see [the website](https://ai-exploration-lab.vercel.app/).

## What is AEL?

Most people use AI as a tool. In AEL the AI is what you study. Does a model read
the text or the party label? Can it tell which Broadway shows have staying
power? Does its account of a diplomatic episode shift based on who it thinks is
asking?

Every project starts from a **Research Kit**: a focused question, a frozen
dataset we hand you, and three study designs you can choose from. From there you
design a controlled experiment, score the model against a record that already
exists, document every run in a spreadsheet, and turn the findings into a poster.

The comparison always comes from data that somebody already collected and
published: coded court records, hand-labelled sentences, results that are a
matter of public record. You measure a model against ground truth, and that is
what makes this research.

**The Removal Test:** if you remove AI from your project and the research
question still makes sense, the project needs reframing. Study AI as a research
subject, not an oracle.

## Questions

**Which AI tools do I use?**
You run models on the machines in McGraw's Digital Learning Lab, in Lewis
Library. The co-leads confirm the exact models and versions at the first
workshop.

**What if my semester gets busy?**
Tell a co-lead. The scope and the timeline of your project can be adjusted.
