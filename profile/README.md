# AI Exploration Lab

**Princeton Data and Intelligent Systems · Fall 2026**

> You aren't just using AI to do research. You're doing research on AI.

Welcome. The AI Exploration Lab (AEL) is a semester-long program where ~30 Princeton undergraduates investigate AI as a *research subject* across every discipline on campus. You pick a Research Kit in your field, make the question your own, run real experiments, and present what you found at the December symposium.

🌐 **Website:** [ai-exploration-lab.vercel.app](https://ai-exploration-lab.vercel.app/)

## What is AEL?

Most people use AI as a tool. AEL flips it: the AI is the thing under the microscope. Does a model read the text or the party label? Can it tell which Broadway shows have staying power? Does its account of a diplomatic episode shift based on who it thinks is asking?

Every project starts from a **Research Kit**: a focused question, a frozen dataset we hand you, and three directions you can take it. From there you design a controlled experiment, score the model against a record that already exists, document every run in a spreadsheet, and turn the findings into a poster.

That last point is what makes this research rather than opinion. The comparison always comes from data somebody already collected and published: coded court records, hand-labelled sentences, results that are a matter of public record. You are measuring a model against a known answer.

**The Removal Test:** if you remove AI from your project and the research question still makes sense, the project needs reframing. Study AI as a research subject, not an oracle.

## Who can apply

All Princeton undergrads. Any major, any year.

- **No coding required.** Spring 2026 projects spanned creative writing, music, classroom interventions, art history, and language pedagogy alongside technical work. Every kit can be done by hand in a chat window.
- **No AI background required.** Roughly half the previous cohort had never worked with AI before. The first workshops cover idea-to-execution and tool setup.
- **Non-STEM welcome.** Most spring projects came from public policy, history, English, music, philosophy, and other non-STEM fields.
- **Freshmen through seniors.** Previous cohorts spanned all four years.

**Time commitment:** a few hours a week. Bi-weekly cohort work sessions (with dinner) and bi-weekly mentor check-ins are scheduled. Project work is at your own pace.

## FAQ

**What AI tools do I get access to?**
This is being finalized with the McGraw Digital Learning Lab and will be confirmed before the first workshop. Ask a co-lead if you need to plan around it.

**What if I get really busy mid-semester?**
Tell us. Scope can adjust and timelines can extend. The program adapts around your availability.

**What kind of work will I produce?**
A brief midpoint reflection, a "go deeper" assignment, and a final poster for the December symposium. The poster is the primary deliverable.

**Can my project count toward a class, thesis, or other work?**
No. AEL projects are standalone passion work, not affiliated with coursework, theses, junior papers, or extracurriculars. This avoids honor code conflicts and keeps the work distinct. It is also what makes the work free: nothing here is graded, so a null result costs you nothing and abandoning a direction in week four costs you nothing.

**Does this need IRB approval?**
No. No project involves human participants. You compare a model against data that already exists and was published by someone else.

**Do I have to use the kits?**
No. You can propose your own project using the same structure. The catalogue has a guide for it.
