# AI Exploration Lab

**Princeton Data and Intelligent Systems · Fall 2026**

> You aren't just using AI to do research. You're doing research on AI.

Welcome. The AI Exploration Lab (AEL) is a semester-long program where ~30 Princeton undergraduates investigate AI as a *research subject* across every discipline on campus. You pick a Research Kit in your field, make the question your own, run real experiments, and present what you found at the December symposium.

🌐 **Website:** [ai-exploration-lab.vercel.app](https://ai-exploration-lab.vercel.app/)

---

## What is AEL?

Most people use AI as a tool. AEL flips it: the AI is the thing under the microscope. Does a model read the text or the party label? Can it tell which Broadway shows have staying power? Does its account of a diplomatic episode shift based on who it thinks is asking?

Every project starts from a **Research Kit**: a focused question, a frozen dataset we hand you, and three directions you can take it. From there you design controlled experiments, build a human baseline, document everything in a spreadsheet, and turn the findings into a poster.

**The Removal Test:** if you remove AI from your project and the research question still makes sense, the project needs reframing. Study AI as a research subject, not an oracle.

## Who can apply

All Princeton undergrads. Any major, any year.

- **No coding required.** Spring 2026 projects spanned creative writing, music, classroom interventions, art history, and language pedagogy alongside technical work. Mentors can help if your project needs code.
- **No AI background required.** Roughly half the previous cohort had never worked with AI before.
- **Non-STEM welcome.** Most spring projects came from public policy, history, English, music, philosophy, and other non-STEM fields.
- **Freshmen through seniors.** Seniors often connect projects to thesis interests. Freshmen use it as a first research experience.

**Time commitment:** a few hours a week. Cohort sessions and mentor check-ins are on a set cadence, project work is at your own pace.

## FAQ

**Do I need to know how to code?**
No. Spring 2026 projects spanned creative writing, music, classroom interventions, art history, and language pedagogy alongside technical work. If your project needs code, mentors can help.

**Do I need an AI background?**
No. Roughly half the previous cohort had never worked with AI before. The first workshops cover idea-to-execution and tool setup.

**What if my major isn't STEM?**
Most spring projects came from non-STEM disciplines: public policy, history, English, music, philosophy. Every discipline has compelling AI questions.

**What's the time commitment?**
A few hours a week. Bi-weekly cohort work sessions (with dinner) and bi-weekly mentor check-ins are scheduled. Project work is at your own pace.

**What AI tools do I get access to?**
Every member gets $50 in tool credits to run experiments across AI models. Mentors can help arrange more if your project needs it.

**Can I apply as a senior or a freshman?**
Yes. Previous cohorts spanned all four years. Seniors often tie projects to thesis interests. Freshmen use it as their first research experience.

**What if I get really busy mid-semester?**
Tell us. Scope can adjust and timelines can extend. The program adapts around your availability.

**What kind of work will I produce?**
A brief midpoint reflection, a "go deeper" assignment, and a final poster for the December symposium. The poster is the primary deliverable.

**Can my project count toward a class, thesis, or other work?**
No. AEL projects are standalone passion work, not affiliated with coursework, theses, junior papers, or extracurriculars. This avoids honor code conflicts and keeps the work distinct.
